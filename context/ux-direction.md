# ux-direction.md

## General UX Philosophy

How should the site FEEL to use?

- smooth
- calm
- responsive
- satisfying yet not gimmicky
- real-futuristic not 2000's interpretation
- medium-lightweight
- intentional

---

## Accessibility Philosophy

How important is accessibility?


- accessibility should never feel like an afterthought
- interactions should still work without hover
- older/non-technical users should still have a form of understanding the site - however a lot of the content isnt necessarily relevant so i wish to convey things in a manner of metaphors so at a glance you get the gist but at closer inspection you get more advanced info
- animations should respect reduced motion - overly animated is tacky

---

## Interaction Philosophy

What kind of interactions should exist?

- subtle hover feedback
- micro-interactions
- dynamic but restrained
- responsive transitions
- hidden details - Hidden details should reward curiosity without becoming essential to navigation or understanding.

What should NOT exist?

- excessive animations
- distracting movement
- flashy gimmicks
- overwhelming transitions

---

## Mobile UX Direction

How should the mobile experience behave?

- mobile-first thinking - as the majority of viewers are on mobile
- touch-friendly interactions
- avoid tiny buttons
- maintain desktop quality on mobile
- avoid cramped layouts
- smooth scrolling


---

## Animation & Motion

What role should motion play?

- subtle enhancement only
- reinforce responsiveness
- should feel smooth not flashy
- should support immersion
- motion should never harm usability

---

## Layout Philosophy

What should layouts feel like?

- spacious
- balanced
- readable
- modular
- organised
- clean but not empty

What should be avoided?

- dashboard clutter
- giant walls of text
- cramped UI
- too many competing elements
- overlapping elements

---

## Typography Philosophy

How should text/content feel?

- readable first
- technically informed
- concise
- visually balanced
- clear hierarchy
- approachable expertise

---

## Visual Density

The site should feel:

- data-rich
- immersive
- spacious
- organised

Although the site is primarily a single-page experience, it should not feel like a traditional scrolling portfolio.

Sections should feel distinct and purposeful while remaining connected as part of a cohesive experience.

---

## Exploration & Discovery

The site should reward curiosity.

Users should discover:

- subtle hidden details
- evolving content
- interactive reveals
- dynamic live data
- changing activity feeds
- deeper layers of information

Additional depth should be available to curious visitors without making the primary experience confusing or difficult to understand.

The site should reveal more of itself over time.

---

## UX Anti-Goals

What should the UX NEVER become?

Examples:

- overwhelming
- confusing
- noisy
- corporate dashboard
- generic SaaS
- gimmicky
- inaccessible
- visually exhausting
